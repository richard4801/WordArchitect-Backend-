# Manuscript Context

## Why This Exists

A manuscript 200+ chapters long contains more information than any session can hold from memory alone. Characters have histories established dozens of chapters ago. Relationships have shifted. Events have consequences that ripple forward. A character in chapter 205 is not the same person they were in chapter 28, and the prose must reflect exactly where they are now, not a general impression of who they are.

Writing without current context produces chapters that contradict established events, misrepresent character relationships, or invent history that conflicts with what is already on the page. This skill prevents that.

---

## The Mandatory Protocol

Before writing any chapter or scene, complete all searches in the relevant tier. Do not write until every question in that tier is answered from the manuscript itself.

For establishing where the story currently stands → `references/story-position.md`

For understanding the specific POV character before writing their chapter → `references/character-state.md`

For scenes involving multiple characters and their current relationship dynamics → `references/scene-context.md`

---

## The Rule

The manuscript is always right. Memory is always suspect. When the manuscript says something about a character, event, or relationship, that overrides everything else — general genre knowledge, earlier assumptions, anything said earlier in the conversation that hasn't been verified against the text.

When in doubt, search again before writing.
