# Scene Context — Grounding Every Scene in What Actually Exists

## When to Use This

Before writing any scene involving more than one character, or any scene that references a specific place, event, or object that exists in the manuscript. The purpose is to ensure the scene sits accurately in the world that has already been built.

---

## The Searches

### 1. The Immediate Preceding Events

**Search: `chapter [number] events [character]`**

Every scene follows something. The characters in it are carrying whatever just happened to them. Before writing the scene, establish:

- What happened in the chapter or scene immediately before this one?
- What did each character in the scene just come from?
- What is each of them carrying into this room?

A scene written without this knowledge produces characters who behave as if the preceding chapters didn't happen.

---

### 2. The Physical Space

**Search: `[location] description room keep fortress`**

If a scene takes place in a specific location that has appeared in the manuscript before, search for how that location has been described. Ironmaw's great hall, Sera's quarters, the council chamber, the dungeon cells — all of these have established details. The scene should be consistent with those details.

If the location hasn't been established yet, note that and build it fresh — but consistently with the broader physical world of Ironmaw or Valdris as already described.

---

### 3. The Object or Event Being Referenced

**Search: `[specific object or event]`**

If the scene involves something specific — a recording crystal, a marriage parchment, Bram's face graft, the Totem in Torin's heart — search for every previous reference to that thing before writing it into the scene. Confirm:

- What does this thing look like?
- Who knows about it?
- What is its current status?
- Has anything changed about it recently?

Objects and events that span multiple chapters are where continuity errors most commonly occur.

---

### 4. The Relationship at This Exact Moment

**Search: `[character A] [character B] recent scene last interaction`**

Before writing two characters together, find the last scene they shared and establish the current state of their relationship from that point. Not from general knowledge of the relationship — from the most recent contact between them.

The most important version of this search for this manuscript:

- `Sera Fenris most recent scene`
- `Sera Mina most recent scene`
- `Aldric Irina most recent scene`
- `Fenris council recent`

The relationship right now may be different from the relationship three chapters ago. Write from now.

---

### 5. What Each Character Wants From This Scene

Before writing any scene with dialogue or conflict, answer for each character present:

- What does this character want from this specific conversation or encounter?
- What are they afraid of finding out?
- What are they afraid of revealing?
- What would they never say, and why?

These answers come from the manuscript — from what has been established about each character's desires, fears, and current situation. They cannot be invented from general impressions.

---

## The Scene Foundation Summary

After completing all relevant searches, produce a brief scene foundation before writing. It should answer:

- Where are we, and what does this place look and feel like?
- Who is in this scene, and what has each of them just come from?
- What does each character want from this scene?
- What objects or events relevant to the scene have already been established?
- What is the current state of the relationship between the characters?
- What is the scene trying to do — what should be different by the end of it from what it was at the beginning?

That last question is essential. A scene that ends in the same place it began has failed regardless of how well it is written. Something must shift. A piece of information lands, a relationship changes weight, a decision is made, a crack appears in something that looked solid. The scene foundation must identify what that shift is before writing begins.
