 2. The standing workflow, step by step
 This is the order I follow for every chapter. Each step marked (rule) is a standing instruction from
 you.

  1. Manuscript context first (rule, 18 Nov). Before carding or drafting I run the
    manuscript-context protocol: where the story stands, the POV character's current state, and the
    relationships in the scene, all checked against the text with search_manuscript and
    get_manuscript_chapter. The manuscript beats memory. Every agent follows this, including the
    gatekeeper.
  2. Timeline and travel times. I check the timeline log and the ledger notes so the day, time of
    day and who is where all hold.
  3. Plot check with you. I lay out what the page supports, a rough shape, and the questions the
    checks raise. Per your preference I flag plot problems the moment I see them and offer ideas.
    Example for 594: breakfast clashed with the afternoon timing in 587, and Mina being in bed in
    the afternoon needed a reason.
  4. Card in a Claude Doc (MOMENT pattern). POV, time, place, who is present, who is off the
    page, likely length, the hard facts Hanami must hold, the moments M1, M2 and so on in order,
    the card's own rules, and the items still open.
  5. Gatekeeper on the card. I send the card to the gatekeeper (resumed by SendMessage so it
    keeps its context). Its fixes go into the card, and I report them to you.
  6. Show you the exact Hanami prompts (rule). Nothing goes to Hanami until you have seen
    the prompts and said "Go".

 5. How a Hanami call is built
 The three fields
  • userId (required): your ID. It makes WordArchitect apply your banned-terms list (paragraphs
    that contain a banned term are regenerated automatically) and your Writing Voice profile.
  • compiledContext: everything Hanami knows. Because it is stateless, this must carry the POV
    and tense, who the characters are, the hard facts, the rules, and the story so far.
  • sceneBeat: what to write in this call.


 The structure I settled on in 594 and 595
 After the first attempts failed (section 7), this layout held reliably:
   compiledContext:
   Narrator: Jace, first person (I, me), PAST TENSE. <- tense spelled out
   [who he is, where he is, what he knows right now]
   [hard facts for this beat, stated as facts]
   No dashes. No similes. Never use felt, realized, suddenly. <- rules every call
   No invented names, hair or eye colours. No one-line paragraphs.
   [colour note: this is HIS moment; give his angle, not hers]

   Story so far:
   [the last paragraph or two of ACCEPTED prose, verbatim] <- the anchor

   sceneBeat:
   Continue, past tense, first person, [N] paragraphs, then STOP.
   [the beat as shape: what happens and what he notices, in order]
   STOP [exact stop point]. [what must NOT happen yet]

 Principles behind it
  • One beat per call. Hanami's scope discipline drops sharply past about one paragraph's worth of
    instruction.
  • An anchor in real prose. Ending compiledContext with actual accepted sentences, not a
    summary, keeps Hanami in first-person past tense. A summary as "story so far" made it run on
    into new scenes.
  • The beat in sceneBeat, not buried in the context. Early in 594 I put the beat inside
    compiledContext with sceneBeat "Continue the story." It then treated the beat as already done
    and wrote past it. Moving the beat into sceneBeat fixed this.
  • Explicit STOP and "not yet" lines, for example "STOP on him knowing her. Do not describe
    any smell yet. Do not have anyone cut her yet."
  • Facts stated as facts. Example: "The woman is STILL FULLY CLOTHED... she is not naked."
    Without it Hanami stripped her a beat early.
  • Shape, not script (your rule). I give what happens and what he notices, and leave the
    wording to Hanami. Dialogue already on the page, or set by you, is quoted exactly.
  • Restate the bans every call. Hanami invents small physical details unless told not to, every
    time.

 13. Craft rules applied to every call and fix
  • First person, past tense. No dashes, except a dash for cut-off dialogue; ellipses for trailing words.
  • No felt, realized or suddenly; no stock phrases; figures of speech only if fresh (you reversed the
    blanket simile ban).
  • No invented eye or hair colours, names or history. A POV character can't name places nobody
    told them about, and can't see a look, only what a face does.


Claude × Hanami drafting process — Grace of the Barbarian                                                       6
  • Secrets show as shape until another character's mouth lands them; the narrator doesn't
    conclude a reveal for the reader.
  • A blow needs a trigger and the other person's reaction, written through the body. Every turn gets
    a beat of stillness.
  • No filler that re-narrates. Recognition is a process. Each POV colours the moment; don't re-run
    another POV's scene.
  • Chapters of about 800–1,300 words; splits are welcome.
  • Explicit menace and nudity are allowed in the torture scenes, but Claude won't write a sexual
    assault being carried out.



 14. Limits and what I can't verify
  • Which model is Hanami. WordArchitect's tool says it sends to Hanami, but the response
    carries no model name. The Planning Engine roles list Claude models, but none of them writes
    prose. The evidence that it is Hanami: calls failed when your Hanami subscription lapsed and
    resumed when you renewed it, and the output behaves like a smaller local model.
  • Logging. The draft session records one consolidated iteration for 594 rather than every call.
    Section 10 of this document is the full call record.
  • Hand-fixing versus resending. I sometimes fix by hand where another Hanami call might
    have worked. If you prefer more of the wording to stay Hanami's, I can send broken passages
    back to it, at the cost of more calls.
